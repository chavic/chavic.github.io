~/work/uniffi-dart/challengeThe Dart backend lagged behind Swift/Kotlin: collections were patchy, traits weren’t exposed, callbacks were brittle, and integer handling around 64-bit values was easy to get wrong. The goal was to close those gaps so serious Rust libraries (like BDK) could lean on UniFFI-Dart without bespoke glue.
~/work/uniffi-dart/approach
How I build bindingsI focused on tightening correctness and ergonomics first, surrounding risky paths with fixtures/tests, and shipping CI + tooling so teams can rely on it. Here are a few work samples
Collections & traits that actually round-tripAdded HashMap/Map support, fixed object-key handling, and exposed UniFFI trait interfaces with helper overrides plus a macro-driven fixture to keep coverage honest.
Callbacks & converters that match FFI realityRealigned callback handles and trampolines with expected FFI signatures, fixed optional/sequence converters and flat enums, and patched the bytes converter to respect view bounds.
Runtime & safety passesSwapped Tokio for a leaner smol runtime, enforced bounds for integer primitives (documented temporary u64 handling), and activated a type-limits fixture so regressions show up in CI.
~/work/uniffi-dart/impact
Generated bindings power production Flutter apps for cryptography-heavy workloads.
Automated CI cross-compiles Rust artifacts for Android and iOS in under eight minutes.
Cut integration regressions with contract tests that mirror upstream UniFFI schemas.
Dart bindings can drive real Rust workloads (maps, traits, callbacks, big integers) without custom forks.
Runtime + converter fixes reduced “works in Rust, breaks in Dart” bugs for downstream projects like BDK-Dart.
Fixture and testing work gives maintainers a safety net as UniFFI evolves instead of relying on manual QA.
